Niklas Gruhn, on relaying AI output verbatim:

> By all means, prompt AI. But don't just relay the output. Read it, understand it, validate it, and then write a response in your own words (a decent certificate that you've done the prior steps). Making that effort is value you can add.

This reminds me of [Simon Willison's rule](https://simonwillison.net/2023/Aug/27/wordcamp-llms/#:~:text=One%20of%20my%20rules%20is%20that%20I%20will%20not%20publish%20anything%20that%20takes%20someone%20else%20longer%20to%20read%20than%20it%20took%20me%20to%20write.), <q>I will not publish anything that takes someone else longer to read than it took me to write.</q>

Among friends I'll sometimes skip the rewrite and share a link with the prompt in it instead. As [Dave Rupert](https://daverupert.com/2026/04/claude-no/) mentioned, he wants the prompt because it shows the context you gave the machine and your understanding of the problem: <q>I'd rather have the original prompt so I can just ask the machine myself.</q> It's the least bad way to relay, not an alternative to doing the work.
